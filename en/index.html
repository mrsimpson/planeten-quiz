Planet Quiz

Ten questions about the planets of our Solar System: structure, sizes, temperatures, orbital periods and moons. For grades 3 to 5.

Answer the questions one by one. After each answer you see whether it is right and learn more about every answer. At the end you see your result.

Questions

  1. Structure: Put the planets in order of their distance from the Sun. The planet closest to the Sun comes first.

    Give each entry its place: 1 for the first.

    Right answer: 1. Mercury, 2. Venus, 3. Earth, 4. Mars, 5. Jupiter, 6. Saturn, 7. Uranus, 8. Neptune

    Eight planets orbit the Sun. Earth is the third from the Sun, and Mars comes after it and before Jupiter. A sentence made from the first letters helps to remember the order.

    More on it:

    • Solar System (Wikipedia)

  2. Structure: Which planets are rocky planets, made of solid rock?

    • Venus right answer

      Venus is a rocky planet. It is almost as big as Earth and has a solid surface.

    • Mars right answer

      Mars is a rocky planet. Its surface is made of rock and red dust.

    • Jupiter

      Jupiter is a gas giant. It is made mostly of hydrogen and helium and has no solid surface like Earth.

    • Neptune

      Neptune is an ice giant. It is very far from the Sun and has no solid surface like Earth.

    The four inner planets Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars are rocky planets. Jupiter and Saturn are gas giants, and Uranus and Neptune are ice giants.

    More on it:

    • Solar System (Wikipedia)

  3. Sizes: Which planet is the biggest in the Solar System?

    • Jupiter right answer

      Jupiter is a gas giant. Its diameter is more than eleven times that of Earth.

    • Saturn

      Saturn is the second biggest planet. With its rings it looks huge, but without them it is smaller than Jupiter.

    • Neptune

      Neptune is an ice giant and the outermost planet. It is much smaller than Jupiter.

    • Earth

      Earth is the biggest of the four rocky planets. Next to the gas giants, though, it is small.

    Jupiter is the biggest planet. The smallest is Mercury.

    More on it:

    • Jupiter (Wikipedia)
    • Mercury (Wikipedia)

  4. Sizes: About how many times does Earth fit into the diameter of Jupiter? Being off by up to 1 still counts as correct.

    Right answer: 11 times (allowed difference: 1 times)

    Jupiter's diameter is about 143,000 kilometres and Earth's is about 12,700 kilometres. Divided, that gives about 11.

    More on it:

    • Jupiter (Wikipedia)

  5. Temperatures: Which planet is the hottest?

    • Venus right answer

      On Venus it is about 464 °C on average. Its thick air of carbon dioxide traps the heat.

    • Mercury

      Mercury is closest to the Sun, but it has hardly any atmosphere. By day it gets up to about 430 °C, at night it drops to about −170 °C.

    • Mars

      Mars is about −60 °C on average. Its air is thin and stores hardly any heat.

    • Jupiter

      Jupiter is far from the Sun. Its clouds are very cold.

    Venus is hotter than Mercury even though it is farther from the Sun. The reason is the greenhouse effect of its thick atmosphere.

    More on it:

    • Venus (Wikipedia)

  6. Temperatures: On Mars it is warmer on average than on Earth.

    Right answer: The statement is false.

    That is not true. On Mars it is about −60 °C on average, on Earth about 15 °C. Mars is farther from the Sun and has only thin air.

    More on it:

    • Mars (Wikipedia)

  7. Orbital periods: What is a year on a planet?

    • One trip around the Sun right answer

      Every planet needs a different time for one trip around the Sun. That is its year.

    • One spin around its own axis

      One spin around its own axis makes a day, not a year.

    • One trip of a moon around its planet

      That is the orbital period of a moon. On Earth we call it a month.

    • Always 365 days

      A year on Earth lasts about 365 days. On other planets a year is shorter or longer.

    A year is the time for one trip around the Sun. Every planet has a different path and speed, so each has a year of its own length.

    More on it:

    • Year (Wikipedia)

  8. Orbital periods: Put the planets in order of the length of their year. The shortest year comes first.

    Give each entry its place: 1 for the first.

    Right answer: 1. Mercury, 2. Mars, 3. Jupiter, 4. Saturn

    The farther a planet is from the Sun, the longer its path and the slower it moves. So its year is longer: Mercury needs about 88 days, Mars about 687 days, Jupiter about 12 Earth years and Saturn about 29 Earth years.

    More on it:

    • Year (Wikipedia)
    • Saturn (Wikipedia)

  9. Moons: Which planets have no moon?

    • Mercury right answer

      Mercury has no moon. It is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun.

    • Venus right answer

      Venus has no moon, even though it is almost as big as Earth.

    • Earth

      Earth has one moon. It goes around Earth in about a month.

    • Mars

      Mars has two small moons, Phobos and Deimos.

    Mercury and Venus are the only planets without a moon. All other planets have at least one.

    More on it:

    • Mercury (Wikipedia)
    • Venus (Wikipedia)

  10. Moons: How many moons does Mars have?

    Right answer: 2 moons

    Mars has two small moons, Phobos and Deimos. They are irregularly shaped, a bit like potatoes.

    More on it:

    • Mars (Wikipedia)
